Most small businesses don’t fail at social media for lack of talent or budget. They trip over the same few mistakes, which creep in almost by themselves in the rush of day-to-day business.

The good news: none of these mistakes is dramatic, and almost all of them can be fixed with a little structure. Here are the eight we come across most often in our work with local businesses, each with a specific remedy.

## 1. Posting without a goal or a plan

The classic: you post because that’s simply what everyone does. Without a goal, though, it remains unclear which content makes sense and whether the effort is worth it at all.

**How to do it better:** Formulate one single main goal that relates to your business, for example more appointment enquiries for a particular service or more guests at lunchtime. Derive three to five topic areas from it. You will find a guide to this in our article on [social media strategy for small businesses](post:social-media-strategy-small-business).

## 2. Posting irregularly

A post every day for two weeks, then six weeks of radio silence. This pattern almost always arises when posts are put together spontaneously in passing. To visitors, a profile whose last post is months old quickly looks as though the business is no longer active at all.

**How to do it better:**

- Choose a rhythm that is manageable even in stressful periods. One post a week that actually appears beats five planned ones that never happen.
- Produce in advance: set aside one to two hours once a month for photos and videos.
- Use the platform’s scheduling function so that posts appear automatically.

## 3. Showing nothing but advertising

“New in”, “Offer of the week”, “Appointments still available”: if a profile consists exclusively of advertising, people have little reason to follow it. Social media is not a brochure; it is more like a shop window with an open view into the shop.

**How to do it better:** Mix your content. As a rough guide, which you should check against your own figures, advertising in the narrower sense should make up the smaller share. Fill the rest with:

- **Glimpses behind the scenes:** How is the dish of the day made? What does a morning in the studio look like?
- **People:** Who is behind the counter? Who cuts the hair?
- **Useful content:** care tips, short how-tos, answers to frequently asked questions.
- **Results:** finished work, happy moments – with the consent of the people shown.

## 4. An incomplete profile

Many people visit your profile before they come to you for the first time. If the address, opening hours or a way of getting in touch is missing, you lose potential customers at a point where they were actually almost convinced.

Check your profile against this checklist:

- Name and profile picture are clearly recognisable, ideally with your logo or shopfront.
- The description says in one sentence what you offer and where.
- The address, a contact button and a working link for bookings or reservations are in place.
- Opening hours, prices or the menu are easy to find, for instance in the highlights.
- The details match your website and your listing in map search.
- The Legal Notice (Impressum) can be reached. You should take legal advice on what applies to you specifically here.

## 5. Leaving messages and comments unanswered

A reservation enquiry by direct message that goes unanswered for three days is a lost reservation. For many customers, in-app messages are now as natural as a phone call.

**How to do it better:**

1. Decide who in the team is responsible for messages and comments.
2. Set two fixed times a day for a quick check.
3. Save reply templates for frequent questions such as prices, opening hours or booking appointments.
4. Respond to praise and simple comments too – a brief thank-you is enough.

## 6. Chasing trends that don’t fit

Not every trending sound and not every challenge suits a tax consultancy or a medical supplies shop. If you copy trends that have no connection to your own business, the best you can hope for is views from people who will never become customers.

**How to do it better:** Ask yourself two questions before every trend. Does it suit our tone? Can it be linked to what we offer? Only if both apply is it worth doing. Also bear in mind that separate rules apply to business accounts when using music, and you should check these beforehand.

## 7. Looking at the wrong numbers

Followers and likes are visible and feel good, but say little about whether social media is helping your business. A post with few likes can trigger several appointment enquiries, while another with many likes brings in none at all.

**How to do it better:** Pay attention to signals that are closer to revenue: saved and shared posts, profile visits, link clicks and, above all, actual enquiries. And simply ask new customers how they found you. There is more on this in the article [Measuring the success of social media marketing](post:measuring-social-media-success).

## 8. Giving up too soon

After four weeks without a noticeable effect, the channel is abandoned again. That is understandable, but often too soon. Visibility and trust come from repetition, and that takes time. Exactly how long cannot be credibly predicted.

**How to do it better:** Commit to a fixed period of at least three months in which you stick to your plan consistently. Once a month, evaluate what worked and change just one thing at a time. That way you learn step by step what goes down well with your customers.

## Quick check: where do you stand?

Go through the following questions honestly. Every “no” is a concrete starting point for the coming weeks.

- Can I say in one sentence what social media should achieve for my business?
- Has at least one post appeared every week over the last four weeks?
- Do my last nine posts show more than just offers?
- Can a stranger find the address, contact details and a way to book on my profile within a few seconds?
- Are messages answered within one working day?
- Do I know how many enquiries came in via social media last month?
- Do I have consent from everyone who is shown?

You don’t have to solve everything at once. Pick the two points that are quickest to fix and work your way on from there.
