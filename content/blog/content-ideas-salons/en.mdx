Hardly any industry has it as good on social media as hairdressers and beauty salons. Visible results are created in your salon every day, there is real craftsmanship to see, and your clients constantly ask you questions that interest other people too.

Even so, almost every salon owner knows the feeling: the day is full, the phone is in the drawer, and in the evening nothing comes to mind. This article gives you a stock of ideas, sorted by topic area, and shows you how to build them into everyday salon life without much extra effort.

## Before you film: consent and light

There are two things you should sort out before you get started.

**Consent.** Before-and-after pictures are personal images. Before taking them, ask every client whether you may take and publish photos or videos, and record their consent. A short form at reception has proved effective. Anyone who doesn’t want to be filmed isn’t filmed – no discussion. You should have what exactly is legally required checked for your business. The same goes for music in videos: separate rules apply to business accounts.

**Light and background.** Set up a fixed spot for photos and videos: close to the window or with a ring light, in front of a plain wall. That way hair colours look truer, your pictures look consistent, and you don’t have to think it through afresh every time.

## Showing results: transformations and details

Results are the most important reason why people follow a salon. They answer the question: “Can they do what I have in mind?”

1. **Before-and-after as a short video:** a few seconds of before, a quick cut, then the result from several angles.
2. **Before-and-after as a carousel:** the result as the first image, followed by the starting point and the steps in between.
3. **The spin:** The client turns once in the chair or shakes out their hair. Simple and effective.
4. **Close-ups:** the colour gradient of a balayage, clean lines on a fade, a close-up of nails or lashes.
5. **One cut, three stylings:** Show how versatile a hairstyle is in everyday life.
6. **How it lasts:** What does the colour look like after a few weeks? That shows quality and honesty.
7. **Corrections:** How you rescue a home dye job gone wrong – without embarrassing anyone, of course.

<Callout title="Tip for more enquiries">
For every result, add what the service is called, roughly how long it takes and how to book it with you. That turns a nice picture into a concrete invitation to enquire about an appointment.
</Callout>

## Sharing knowledge: tips and explanations

Tips let you show your expertise, and posts like these are readily saved and passed on. The best topics come from the questions you hear in consultations.

8. **Care tips for at home:** blow-drying properly, heat protection, how often to wash.
9. **Busting myths:** What is really true about trimming the ends or washing your hair every day?
10. **Explaining treatments:** What is the difference between balayage, highlights and glossing? How does a lash extension appointment work?
11. **Preparing for the appointment:** What should clients bear in mind before a bleaching or a facial?
12. **Product of the month:** Who is it suitable for, and how is it used? Honest and without sales pressure.
13. **Styling in a few steps:** curls with straighteners, a quick bun, a beard neatly shaped.
14. **Seasonal topics:** hair care after the summer holiday, skin care when the heating is on, hairstyles for the school-leavers’ ball (Abiball) or a wedding.

## Offering glimpses: the team and everyday salon life

People go to people. With such a personal service in particular, likeability and trust play a part in the decision. Glimpses behind the scenes also take away the nerves before a first visit.

15. **Introducing the team:** each person in a short video with their specialism and favourite service.
16. **A day in the salon:** from unlocking the door to the last client, as a time-lapse.
17. **Mixing colour:** The work at the colour bar fascinates many viewers.
18. **Further training:** Show when the team attends a seminar or practises a new technique.
19. **Apprentices in focus:** progress on training heads, their first client of their own, exams passed.
20. **The salon itself:** a short tour, new furnishings, the coffee corner.
21. **Honest moments:** the things that go wrong now and then in everyday life, told with a wink.

## Building trust and covering the practicalities

These posts are less glamorous, but they noticeably lower the hurdles before booking.

22. **How to book with us:** explained step by step – by link, phone or message.
23. **The first visit:** How does a consultation work, and what should you bring along – inspiration pictures, for instance?
24. **Frequently asked questions:** How long does each service take, what happens if you are late, how does cancelling work?
25. **Free appointments:** at short notice in the Story – ideal for gaps in the diary.
26. **Client testimonials:** quotes from reviews or short video statements, always with consent.
27. **Promotions and news:** new services, vouchers before public holidays, changed opening hours.

## Which format suits which idea?

| Format | Particularly suitable for | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short video (Reel) | Transformations, craftsmanship, tips, introducing the team | Medium |
| Carousel | Before-and-after with steps in between, how-tos, frequently asked questions | Low to medium |
| Single photo | Finished results, details, announcements | Low |
| Story | Free appointments, everyday life, polls, short-notice information | Very low |

## Your weekly plan as an example

This is what a realistic week could look like. Treat it as a starting point and adjust it based on your insights.

- **Monday:** Story with the week’s free appointments
- **Tuesday:** Reel with a transformation from the weekend
- **Thursday:** carousel with a care tip or a treatment explained
- **Saturday:** photo of a result or a glimpse of the team
- **In between:** two to three spontaneous Stories from everyday salon life

You can read how to find the right frequency for you in the article on [posting frequency on Instagram](post:instagram-posting-frequency).

## How to build content into everyday salon life

The best list of ideas is no use if nobody reaches for their phone during the day. These habits help:

1. **Make the “before” photo a routine.** Straight after the consultation, before you get started. Without a “before” there is no before-and-after.
2. **Use a fixed filming spot.** Every result is captured in the same place.
3. **Name one person.** Someone in the team is responsible for remembering to take photos and videos and for collecting the material.
4. **Store the material in a shared folder,** sorted by date or service.
5. **Plan once a week.** Half an hour is enough to turn the collected material into the coming week’s posts.
6. **Keep an eye on messages.** Anyone who sends an appointment enquiry after a post should get a quick reply.

With this routine, content is created along the way rather than as an extra task after closing time.
