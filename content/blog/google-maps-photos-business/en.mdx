Anyone looking for a restaurant, a hairdresser or a garage nearby often sees images first on Google: the façade, the dining room, a dish. If these photos are old or dark, or still show the previous owner’s furnishings, the result is a false impression – without you noticing anything.

The good news: you can maintain a large part of this yourself. This article explains how it works, where the limits are and how to stay on top of it with little effort.

## What is the Google Business Profile?

The Google Business Profile – formerly “Google My Business” – is your business’s listing on Google. It appears in Google Search and on Google Maps and includes, among other things, the name, address, opening hours, phone number, reviews and photos.

The profile is a free Google product. You can create it or, if a listing already exists, claim it and have yourself verified as the owner. Only then can you manage the details.

## Who can add photos?

Two groups:

- **You and your team:** Owners and managers of the profile can upload photos, a logo and a cover photo and remove their own images again.
- **Customers:** Visitors can also contribute photos to your listing, for example together with a review.

That explains why images you never uploaded often turn up in the profile. It cannot be prevented – but you can make sure there are enough good images of your own.

## How to add photos, a logo and a cover photo

The route in brief: sign in with the Google Account that manages your profile. Then search for your business in Google Search or on Google Maps. As an owner or manager, you will see the management options for your profile there. In the photos section you upload new images and set the logo and cover photo.

The exact wording of the buttons changes time and again and differs between computer and smartphone. If you cannot find a menu item, check Google’s current Help page on managing photos; it is listed under the sources at the end of this article.

- **Logo:** your identifying mark, by which customers recognise you on Google.
- **Cover photo:** the image that, in your view, best represents your business.
- **Further photos:** everything that shows what visitors can expect from you.

### Technical specifications

In its Help pages, Google gives the following guide values for photos:

| Attribute | Google’s specification |
|---|---|
| Format | JPG or PNG |
| File size | between 10 KB and 5 MB |
| Recommended resolution | 720 × 720 pixels |
| Minimum resolution | 250 × 250 pixels |
| Quality | in focus, well lit, without heavy editing or excessive filters |

Current smartphones deliver this resolution without any trouble. Check the values on the Help page before a larger upload all the same, because Google adjusts specifications like these from time to time.

## Which photos help?

Imagine someone who has never been to your business. What does this person want to know before setting off?

- **Exterior view:** façade and entrance, ideally from the direction most people come from. That way people can actually find you.
- **Interiors:** dining room, shop floor, treatment room, waiting area – honest and tidy.
- **Products or dishes:** what customers come to you for. In hospitality, a few typical dishes; in retail, the range.
- **Team:** people build trust. Get the consent of everyone who can be seen beforehand.
- **At work:** the chef at the pass, the mechanic in the workshop, the hairdresser at the chair.

## Practical photo tips

1. **Clean the lens** – the simplest step with the greatest effect.
2. **Use daylight.** For exterior shots, choose a time of day when the façade is not in harsh backlight.
3. **Tidy up.** Bins, boxes, cleaning products and full ashtrays out of the picture.
4. **Hold it straight.** Crooked walls and converging lines look restless.
5. **Stay realistic.** Heavy filters and editing fit neither Google’s specifications nor what guests see on site.
6. **Respect rights.** Photograph customers only with their consent or in such a way that nobody is recognisable.
7. **Keep it current.** After a refurbishment, new furnishings or a new menu, new images belong in the profile.

## What you can control – and what you can’t

| Topic | Your influence |
|---|---|
| Your own photos, logo, cover photo | You upload them and can replace or remove them. |
| Which image appears first | Google decides. Your cover photo is a preference, not a guarantee. |
| Photos from customers | You cannot delete them yourself, but you can report them if they violate guidelines. |
| Order of the remaining images | Determined by Google. |

On the cover photo, Google itself writes that selecting one does not guarantee that it will be displayed as the first image. If an image is of poor quality, a photo uploaded by users may appear instead. A good, meaningful cover photo is therefore the most sensible lever you have.

If a customer photo shows a different business, contains personal data or is obviously spam, you can submit it to Google using the report function on the photo. Google reviews the report; this can take several working days, and there is no entitlement to removal. An unflattering but genuine photo will generally stay.

## Keeping your details up to date

Check regularly:

- **Opening hours,** including public holidays, annual closures and special opening hours
- **Category:** it should describe your core business
- **Description:** short, factual, without advertising clichés
- **Phone number, website and address**
- **Further links,** for instance to reservations or the current menu

Google writes in its Help pages that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to appear in local search results. That is not a promise of a particular position – but it is a good reason to take the details seriously. ## Access for an agency: manager instead of password

If someone else is to maintain your profile, never pass on your Google password. The Business Profile has roles for this:

- **Owners** can manage the profile in full and add or remove other people. There can be several owners, but only one primary owner.
- **Managers** can maintain the profile largely as owners can, but cannot add or remove people or delete the profile.

For an agency, the manager role is the right one: it can maintain photos and details, while you remain the owner and can withdraw the access again at any time. As the owner, you send the invitation in your profile’s settings, in the section for people and access; the person invited has to accept it. Here too, you will find the exact menu names on Google’s current Help page.

## A simple maintenance routine

- **Once a month:** check the opening hours, look at new customer photos, upload two or three current images.
- **With every change:** add a new menu, a new range, a refurbishment or a new phone number straight away.
- **Before public holidays and annual closures:** enter special opening hours.
- **Once a year:** go through all your own photos and remove outdated ones, check the logo and cover photo, review access rights.
