Food is one of the most rewarding topics on Instagram: it looks good, whets the appetite and everyone has an opinion on it. Even so, many restaurant profiles lie neglected – the last photo is from the summer, the opening hours are no longer right, and messages go unanswered for days.

The problem is rarely a lack of talent; it is the packed working day in the restaurant trade. That is why this guide shows you an approach that fits into a normal week between purchasing, service and the books.

## Your profile: the most important answers in a few seconds

Anyone who lands on your profile wants to know quickly whether a visit is worthwhile. Check whether these points are immediately clear:

- **What kind of cuisine?** “Italian”, “Breakfast and cake”, “Vegan bowls” – a few words are enough.
- **Where?** City and neighbourhood in the bio, the address stored in the profile.
- **When?** Current opening hours, including the day you are closed.
- **How to book?** A link to online reservations or your phone number as a contact button.
- **What does it cost, what is on offer?** A Story highlight with the current menu.

Useful highlights for a restaurant include, for example: menu, lunch menu, events, directions and team. Update them as soon as anything changes. An out-of-date lunch menu looks worse than none at all.

## Which content really interests guests

Nothing but photos of plates quickly becomes monotonous. Alternate between four areas:

### Dishes

The classic – ideally presented so that people feel hungry straight away. Movement helps: the sauce running over the dish, the cheese pulling into strings, the steam rising from the soup.

### People

The chef plating up, the bartender shaking a cocktail, the team before the shift starts. People turn a venue into a place you build a relationship with. Get the consent of everyone who can be seen beforehand.

### A look behind the scenes

The fresh dough in the morning, the delivery from the weekly market, the preparations for a large party. Glimpses like these show care without your having to claim it.

### Atmosphere and news

The terrace in the evening sun, the laid table by the window, the new seasonal menu, the Christmas menu. This is where people find out what an evening at your restaurant feels like and why they should come right now.

<Callout title="Example: a weekly plan for a restaurant">
Tuesday: photo of the new dish of the week with a short description. Thursday: a Reel from the kitchen, for example how the pasta is made. Saturday: an atmospheric shot of the dining room with a note about free tables on Sunday. In addition, a Story with the dish of the day on the days you are open. This is a starting point that you adapt to your capacity.
</Callout>

## Showing food at its best with your phone

You don’t need an expensive camera. In practice, these simple steps make the biggest difference:

1. **Clean the lens.** In the kitchen it is almost always greasy – a wipe with a clean cloth works wonders.
2. **Use daylight.** Put the plate by a window. Ceiling lights and flash make food look flat and yellowish.
3. **Shoot straight away.** Salad wilts, foam disappears, sauces form a skin. Take the photo before the dish goes out.
4. **Choose your angle.** From above for pizza, bowls and laid tables; at an angle from the side for burgers, cakes and anything with height.
5. **Tidy up the background.** Cloths, order slips and half-full glasses out of the picture.
6. **Use portrait format.** For Reels and Stories anyway, and in the feed it also makes better use of the screen.
7. **Film briefly.** A few seconds are enough: plating, cutting into it, serving.

## Stories: the tool for day-to-day business

Stories disappear again after a short time and can therefore be spontaneous. For restaurants they are ideal for up-to-the-minute information:

- the dish of the day or the evening’s recommendation
- “Tables still free tonight from 8 pm”
- changed opening hours, annual closure, private functions
- a quick look into the kitchen shortly before service
- polls, for instance on which dessert should go on the new menu

Anything of lasting relevance you then save in a highlight.

## Being found in your local area

A restaurant doesn’t need followers from all over Germany; it needs guests who live, work or are visiting within easy reach. These things help:

- **Location tag:** Link posts and Stories to your restaurant as a location.
- **Local references in the text:** Mention the city or neighbourhood where it fits naturally instead of tacking on long lists of hashtags.
- **Involve the neighbourhood:** Tag the bakery that supplies your bread, the farm your vegetables come from, or the event next door. They often share the post – and you reach their local audience.
- **Encourage guests:** A small note on the menu or on the table with your profile name is enough for guests to tag you in their Stories.

If, beyond that, you want to reach people nearby in a targeted way for an opening or a special offer, paid ads with regional targeting can be a useful addition. The foundation remains a profile that makes people want to visit.

## Messages and comments: this is where reservations come from

Many guests prefer to send a quick message rather than call: “Do you still have room for six on Saturday?” or “Do you have gluten-free options?” Each of these messages is a potential reservation.

- Decide **who** in the team answers messages and **when** – for example once before lunch service and once in the afternoon.
- Use saved replies for recurring questions about reservations, allergens or groups.
- If you don’t accept reservations by message, say so in a friendly way in the bio and point to the right route.
- Respond to comments, including critical ones. A factual reply shows that you take feedback seriously.

## Planning your effort and evaluating results

It is better to plan a little and do it reliably. A fixed half hour a week in which you photograph dishes by the window and shoot two or three short clips is often enough for the whole week. Whether your rhythm is right is something you can best find out for yourself – we describe how in the article [How often should you post on Instagram?](post:instagram-posting-frequency).

Once a month it is worth taking a look at your insights: which posts were saved or shared? How many profile visits and clicks on the reservation link were there? And, quite simply offline: feel free to ask new guests how they heard about you.

Two final notes: if you use music in Reels, check what is permitted for commercial profiles. And only film guests in such a way that they are not recognisable, or obtain their consent beforehand.
