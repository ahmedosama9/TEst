“Do we have to be on TikTok now as well?” We hear this question from owners of local businesses all the time. The honest answer is: not necessarily. Both platforms can work for a café, a hair salon or a gym, but they do so in different ways and with different amounts of effort.

In this article we compare Instagram and TikTok without the hype. You won’t get any audience figures from us, because they change constantly and say little about your particular customers. Instead, we show you how the platforms differ in everyday use and how you can find out for yourself what is worth your while.

## The key differences at a glance

| Criterion | Instagram | TikTok |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formats | Reels, photos, carousels, Stories | Almost exclusively short videos, plus photo series |
| How you are discovered | Via Reels, search and recommendations, but also strongly via existing followers | Mainly via the recommendation feed, even without followers |
| Role of the profile | Digital business card with highlights, contact button and link | Rather secondary; the individual video takes centre stage |
| Tone | Somewhat more curated; aesthetics play a bigger role | More spontaneous, more personal, entertainment first |
| Customer contact | Direct messages and Stories are a familiar route for many customers | Comments are in the foreground |
| Effort | Easy to plan; also manageable with photos and simple formats | Thrives on regular videos and a willingness to be in front of the camera |

The table describes typical patterns from practice, not fixed rules. Features change regularly on both platforms, so it is always worth taking a look at the app itself.

## What makes Instagram strong for local businesses

For many local businesses, Instagram is more than a channel for posts. The profile works like a second website: people who hear about you often drop by there first to get an impression. Are the latest posts up to date? What does your place look like? How do I reach you?

Instagram comes with practical building blocks for this:

- **Several formats:** You don’t have to turn every topic into a video. A carousel with the new lunch menu or a good photo of the finished haircut works too.
- **Stories:** Ideal for day-to-day news such as free appointments, the dish of the day or changed opening hours.
- **Highlights and profile information:** Prices, directions, team and frequently asked questions can be stored where they remain permanently visible.
- **Direct messages:** Many customers ask questions or send enquiries by message as a matter of course.

The downside: if you only post photos and do without Reels, in practice you often reach mainly people who already follow you. If you want to reach new people, there is hardly any way round short videos on Instagram either. ## What TikTok does differently

TikTok is geared consistently to the recommendation feed. There, users mainly see videos from accounts they don’t follow at all. For you that means: even a brand-new account can reach people with a single video. You can’t rely on it, though, and reach there is often erratic. One video does well, the next hardly at all.

The following is also typical of TikTok:

- **Personality beats perfection.** Videos in which someone from the team talks, explains or simply shows what everyday life looks like usually fit better than elaborately produced image films.
- **Entertainment or usefulness first.** A pure advert has a hard time. A look behind the counter or an honest tip from a professional, on the other hand, fits the environment well.
- **Reach is not automatically local.** It can happen that your video reaches many people who will never come to your town. For a restaurant that is worth less than for an online shop. Local references in the video, in the text and in the location details help, but are no guarantee.

So TikTok requires a willingness to film regularly and to experiment to some extent. If you enjoy that, you can build up a lot of visibility there. If you feel uncomfortable in front of the camera, you will find it hard in the long run.

## How to find out where your customers are

Instead of relying on general statements, you can gather your own clues with little effort:

1. **Ask.** For a week, ask at the till or during appointments: “Where are you actually most likely to watch videos?” A tally sheet is enough.
2. **Look around.** Which comparable businesses in your region are active on which platform, and do they visibly get reactions there from real local customers?
3. **Check your own data.** If you already have an Instagram account, the insights show you who you reach and where these people come from.
4. **Search for yourself.** Enter your location and your industry on both platforms. What appears there, and would your content fit in?
5. **Look out for mentions.** Are guests or customers already tagging you somewhere? That is a strong sign of where they are active anyway.

## One platform or both?

The most common wrong decision is not choosing the wrong platform, but too many at once. An abandoned channel looks worse than none at all. These are the three situations we come across most often in practice:

**You have little time and are just starting out.** Concentrate on Instagram. You get a business card, a contact route and reach via Reels in one channel and stay flexible when it comes to formats.

**You already produce short videos regularly anyway.** Then TikTok is possible as a second channel with a manageable amount of extra work. Upload your videos there as well and watch what happens.

**You or someone in the team enjoys being in front of the camera.** Then TikTok may even become your stronger channel. You should still keep Instagram as a well-maintained profile, because many people look there for contact details and a first impression.

## One video, two platforms: how to make double use work

If you want to run both platforms, you don’t have to produce everything twice. Pay attention to these points:

- **Film vertically** and keep important content in the centre of the frame so that nothing is covered by interface elements.
- **Save the original file** before you add text and music in an app. That way you upload a clean version everywhere, without another platform’s watermark.
- **Adapt the text and the opening.** What works in a casual style on TikTok can be a little tidier on Instagram.
- **Check the music.** Separate rules on music use apply to business accounts, and they differ depending on the platform. Clarify this before you use a trending sound.
- **Obtain consent.** If customers or employees can be seen in the video, you need their consent. That applies on every platform.

## Your four-week test

If you are unsure, a limited test helps more than any discussion:

1. **Set a goal.** What should the second platform deliver? For example more profile visits, enquiries or reservations.
2. **Set the scope.** Commit to a realistic number of videos per week and keep it up for four weeks.
3. **Same content, two channels.** Upload the same videos to both platforms, each with adapted text.
4. **Compare after four weeks.** Look at the statistics in both apps: where were the videos seen, where did reactions come from people in your region, and where did enquiries actually arise?
5. **Decide.** Carry on, adjust or deliberately close the second channel again.

Four weeks is not a final verdict, but it is a good starting point.
