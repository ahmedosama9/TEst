“Is this actually doing anything for us?” Sooner or later, everyone who puts time or money into social media asks this question. Many then look at their follower count, aren’t sure what to make of it, and carry on as before.

Yet measuring success is not rocket science for a small business. You don’t need an expensive tool or complicated reports. You need a clear goal, a few suitable key figures and a fixed routine. That is exactly what we build up step by step in this article.

## First the goal, then the key figure

Whether a number is good depends on what you want to achieve. Lots of views are great if you want to become better known in your town. They are of little help if what you actually need is appointment enquiries and nobody clicks on your booking link.

So set one main goal first and then assign key figures to it:

| Goal | Suitable key figures | Other ways you will notice it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local awareness | Reach, views, shared posts | People mention your posts to you in the shop |
| Interest in what you offer | Saved posts, profile visits, comments with questions | More questions about particular services |
| Enquiries and bookings | Link clicks, direct messages, taps on call or directions | Reservations and appointments that can be attributed |
| Customer loyalty | Reactions to Stories, recurring commenters | Regular customers mention your posts |
| Recruiting staff | Reach of job posts, messages from interested people | Applications that refer to social media |

We show you how to formulate a goal like this properly in our article on [social media strategy for small businesses](post:social-media-strategy-small-business).

## The most important key figures, clearly explained

The names in the apps change time and again, and not every platform counts in the same way. So look in the statistics section of your app and go by the meaning, not the exact name.

- **Reach:** How many different accounts have seen your post. It shows whether you are being noticed at all.
- **Views:** How often a post or video was displayed. One person can count several times.
- **Saved posts:** Someone wants to find the content again later. That is a strong sign of real usefulness, for instance with tips, menus or price lists.
- **Shared posts:** Someone passes your post on. For local businesses this is digital word of mouth: “We really must go there.”
- **Profile visits:** People want to know more about you. Often this is the step immediately before an enquiry.
- **Link clicks and contact actions:** Clicks on your booking link, on call, email or directions. These values are closest to actual business.
- **Direct messages:** Count how many genuine enquiries come in by message. The app doesn’t always provide this cleanly; a tally sheet helps.

## What you shouldn’t overrate

**Follower count.** It grows slowly, says little about quality and is easily inflated with the wrong audience. A thousand followers from all over Germany help a restaurant less than two hundred from its own town. It is better to look at your audience details to see whether you are reaching people from your region.

**Likes.** A like is quickly given and has little to do with any intention to buy. A post with the new price list may get hardly any likes, but is often saved and leads to bookings.

**Individual outliers.** A video that suddenly gets a great many views is nice, but not a benchmark. Reach fluctuates widely. Look at the average over several weeks rather than individual peaks or troughs.

**Comparisons with others.** Other people’s averages rarely fit your situation. Your own previous month is the more honest yardstick.

## Recording enquiries and reservations the simple way

The most important question is: is social media actually bringing in customers? For local businesses, much of this happens offline, so a look at the app is not enough. Three simple methods close the gap.

### 1. Asking

The simplest method is often the best. Ask new customers: “How did you hear about us?” You can do this in conversation, on the phone or as a mandatory field in the booking form. Keep a tally sheet at the till or reception with categories such as Instagram, recommendation, search engine and passing trade.

### 2. Links with UTM parameters

If you have a website with an analytics tool, you can tag links. UTM parameters are small additions at the end of a web address that tell your analytics tool where a visitor came from, for instance from your Instagram profile or from a particular Story. Free UTM generators create such links in a few seconds. The only important thing is to keep the naming consistent.

### 3. A dedicated booking route

Many booking and reservation systems allow separate links or show which channel a booking came through. In your profile, use a dedicated link just for social media. Alternatively, a keyword or a small voucher code that you communicate exclusively there will work.

A hair salon notes in the appointment book where every new client has come from. At the end of the month, the team counts the entries marked Instagram and writes the number next to the values from the app. That makes it clear whether the posts are merely being seen or are also bringing in appointments.

None of these methods is watertight. Some customers see you on Instagram, look you up later via a search engine and then call. That is fine: you don’t need perfect attribution, just a reliable trend.

## Your monthly routine in 30 minutes

Figures only help if you look at them regularly and draw something from them. Put a fixed date in your diary, for example the first Monday of the month, and proceed as follows:

1. **Note your key figures.** Enter your two to three most important values for the past month in a simple table, along with the number of enquiries from your tally sheet.
2. **Compare with the previous month.** Is it going up, going down or staying the same? Note possible reasons such as holidays, a promotion or fewer posts.
3. **Look at the three best posts.** Sort by the key figure that matches your goal. What do these posts have in common: topic, format, opening, people in the picture?
4. **Look at the three weakest posts.** Is there a pattern, such as pure advertising or a format that doesn’t go down well?
5. **Decide on one change.** Choose exactly one thing to test next month, for example more posts from your strongest topic area.
6. **Record it briefly.** Two sentences on what you have learnt are enough. After six months this gives you a valuable log.

Your table needs only a few columns: month, number of posts, reach, saved and shared posts, profile visits, link clicks, enquiries and a column for notes.

## Putting figures into context

A few principles help you avoid drawing the wrong conclusions from the values:

- **Think in trends.** One weak month is no drama. Three weak months in a row are a signal.
- **Take the season into account.** An ice cream parlour in January and a gym in January face very different conditions.
- **Don’t change everything at once.** Otherwise you won’t know what made the difference.
- **Factor in the effort.** If an elaborate format achieves hardly any more than a simple one, the simple one is the better choice.
- **Stay realistic.** Social media is one building block among several. Nobody can credibly promise you particular results, but you can observe carefully what is changing for you.
