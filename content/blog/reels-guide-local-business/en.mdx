For many local businesses, short videos are the format they are most wary of: too much effort, too technical, “and nobody here wants to be on camera anyway”. Yet hardly anything is as well suited to showing what happens at your business – how the milk foam turns out right, how long hair becomes a bob, how a class really runs.

This guide takes you through the whole thing once: from the idea and the shoot on your phone to publishing and evaluation.

## Why Reels make sense for local businesses

Reels are often also shown to people who don’t follow you yet. That makes them suitable for being newly discovered, whereas Stories mainly keep you in touch with existing followers. No particular reach can be inferred from this, but experience shows that the chance of being seen beyond your own circle is greatest with videos.

On top of that, moving images convey atmosphere. A photo shows the finished dish; a video shows the hands plating it up, the sound of the pan and the smile as it is served. It is exactly this feeling that often decides whether someone drops by.

## The equipment: less than you think

To get started you need:

- **A smartphone** with a decent camera. Give the lens a quick clean before every shoot.
- **Light:** Daylight by a window is the best and cheapest light source. Position yourself or your subject so that the light comes from the front or at an angle from the side, not from behind.
- **Stability:** A small tripod helps; at a pinch a shelf or a stack of books will do.
- **Sound:** If someone is speaking, find a quiet corner. Where there is a lot of background noise, a simple clip-on microphone is worth having.

You can edit directly in the Instagram app or in one of the common editing apps for phones. You only need more equipment once you notice where the simple set-up is actually holding you back.

## Seven Reel ideas that work in almost any business

You don’t have to dance along to trends. Formats that come straight from your everyday work are more productive:

1. **Before and after:** the hairstyle, the bouquet, the restored piece of furniture, the redesigned shop floor.
2. **How it’s made:** from dough to croissant, from fabric to finished cushion – in a few quick cuts.
3. **One question, one answer:** “How often should you have your ends trimmed?” What customers constantly ask you interests other people too.
4. **A day with us:** unlocking, preparing, first guest, closing time.
5. **New in the range:** unpack it, show it, explain in one sentence who it is for.
6. **Introducing the team:** one person, three short questions.
7. **Three tips:** three deadlift mistakes, three wines for a barbecue, three care tips for curls.

<Callout title="Tip: turn one idea into a series">
If a format goes down well, repeat it with new content. A gym can turn “One exercise, done properly” into a new Reel every week. That saves the search for ideas and makes your profile recognisable.
</Callout>

## Step by step: from the idea to the finished Reel

### 1. Define the core message

Write down in one sentence what should stick after the video: “Our lunch menu changes every week” or “This is what a trial session with us looks like”. Leave out anything that doesn’t contribute to it.

### 2. Plan the opening

The first few seconds decide whether someone scrolls on. Tried-and-tested openings are:

- **showing the result first** and then how it came about
- **asking a question** your target audience really has
- **starting with movement**, such as cutting into the cake or the first snip of the scissors
- **showing a clear line of text** that says what it is about

Avoid long greetings and logo animations at the start.

### 3. Write a short shot list

Note down three to six shots, for example: wide shot of the room, close-up of the hands, detail of the product, finished result, happy guest. With a list, you can shoot in ten minutes what would otherwise take you an hour.

### 4. Film

- film in **portrait format**
- record each shot a few seconds longer than necessary so that you have some leeway when editing
- alternate between **close and wide** – that brings a video to life
- hold the phone steady or put it down and **move the subject**, not the camera
- better to take one shot too many; reshooting takes more effort

### 5. Edit

Cut rigorously. Each shot stays only as long as it shows something new. Remove pauses, run-ups and everything that happens before the actual action. If you are unsure whether a scene is needed, it usually isn’t.

### 6. Add text and sound

Many people watch videos without sound. So show the most important messages as on-screen text and use subtitles when someone is speaking. Make sure text at the edges isn’t covered by the app’s interface elements – you can see this in the preview before publishing.

### 7. Cover image, caption and next step

- **Cover image:** Choose a meaningful still so that the Reel also looks good in your profile grid.
- **Caption:** one to three sentences that add to the content, with a local reference where it fits naturally.
- **Next step:** Say what people can do: reserve a table, book an appointment, drop by, send a message.
- **Location:** Link the Reel to your business as a location.

## Music, people and rights: clarify beforehand

There are three points you should keep in mind before publishing. This is not legal advice, but a pointer to what you need to watch out for:

- **Music:** Commercial profiles often don’t have the same selection of music available as private ones. Only use what is offered to you for business use, or work with original sound.
- **People in the picture:** Obtain the consent of employees, customers and guests before you show them – ideally documented in a verifiable way. Particular care is called for with children.
- **Third-party content:** Brands, works of art or other people’s material may be subject to rights of their own.

If you are unsure, have it checked by an expert before a video goes online.

## Evaluating and getting better

Your first Reel won’t be your best, and that is fine. What matters more is that you take a closer look after a few videos. In the insights you will find, among other things, how often each Reel was watched, saved and shared. Look for patterns rather than individual values:

- Which **topics** repeatedly did better than others?
- Which **openings** worked?
- Which Reels led to **profile visits, messages or bookings**?

Do more of what goes down well with your audience. We explain how to put the figures into context in the article [Measuring social media success](post:measuring-social-media-success). To begin with, one Reel a week is a realistic rhythm; the article [How often should you post on Instagram?](post:instagram-posting-frequency) explains how to find your own pace.

## Film it yourself or have it filmed?

There is a lot you can do yourself, especially spontaneous glimpses of everyday life. Support is worthwhile if you simply don’t have the time in your business, if the videos should have a consistent, high-quality look, or if you want to produce material for several weeks in a single session.

With professional [Reels and video production](page:service:reels-video-production), a team comes to your business, plans the content in advance and takes care of filming and editing. A combination often works best: planned Reels from the professionals, spontaneous Stories from you.

Whatever you decide: start with a simple format and repeat it a few times. Routine comes from filming, not from planning.
