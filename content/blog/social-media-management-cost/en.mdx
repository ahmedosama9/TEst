Anyone looking for prices for social media management finds either no figures at all or a range so wide that it tells them nothing. That is frustrating, but there is a simple reason for it: completely different services lie behind the same term.

In this article we deliberately give no euro amounts for the market, because without context they would be misleading. Instead, you get what really helps you decide: the common pricing models, the factors that drive the price, and a checklist for comparing quotes fairly.

## How is social media management billed?

In practice you will come across three models, often in combination.

| Model | How it works | A good fit if … |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly flat fee | A fixed scope of services per month at a fixed price, usually with a minimum term | you want ongoing management and need predictable costs |
| Project price | A one-off, clearly defined service, for example profile set-up, strategy or a shoot day | you have a specific need or want to test the working relationship first |
| Hourly or daily rate | Billing according to actual time spent | it is about consulting, workshops or tasks that are hard to plan |

For ongoing management, the monthly flat fee is the most widespread. It is predictable for both sides – provided the scope is described precisely.

## What drives the price?

When two quotes are far apart, it is almost always down to one of these points:

- **Number of channels:** Just Instagram, or Facebook, TikTok and others as well? Each channel needs its own formats and upkeep, even if some content can be used more than once.
- **Volume and type of content:** A photo post is quicker to create than a Reel with concept, filming, editing and subtitles. The number of posts and Reels per month is usually the biggest lever.
- **On-site shoots:** If someone comes to your business regularly, there are costs for time, equipment and travel. In return you get material that really looks like you.
- **Community management:** Are comments and messages answered for you? How quickly, and on which days?
- **Ads:** Setting up, managing and evaluating campaigns is a separate service – in addition to the advertising budget itself.
- **Strategy and reporting:** Is there a concept, a content calendar, regular reports and catch-up meetings?
- **Contract term:** Longer terms often come with more favourable conditions, but also tie you in for longer.

A hair salon wants one Instagram channel with a few posts a month; the team supplies the images itself. A restaurant wants Instagram and Facebook, weekly Reels from the kitchen, a fixed on-site shoot date and replies to all messages. Both are looking for “social media management” – the amount of work behind it is completely different.

## Freelancer, agency or do it yourself?

None of the three options is the best in principle. It depends on what you need and what you can contribute yourself.

| Criterion | Freelancer | Agency | In-house |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strengths | personal contact, often flexible | several specialists for concept, filming, editing and management; cover in case of absence | maximum closeness to the business, spontaneous content |
| Risks | unavailable during illness or holidays, rarely all disciplines in one person | higher fixed costs, possibly changing points of contact | costs working time, expertise has to be built up |
| A good fit if … | the scope is small and clear | you want reliable management with content production | someone in the team has the time, talent and enthusiasm for it |

With the in-house solution, don’t underestimate the real costs: working hours, training, equipment and the tasks that are left undone during that time. “Doing it yourself” isn’t free either – it just doesn’t appear on any invoice.

## Checklist: what a good quote should contain

A reputable quote is specific enough for you to compare it with another. Check these points:

1. **Quantities:** How many posts, Reels and Stories per month are included?
2. **Channels:** Which platforms are managed?
3. **Production:** Who creates photos and videos? How often is there an on-site shoot, and is travel included?
4. **Coordination:** How do approvals work, and how many rounds of revisions are included?
5. **Community management:** To what extent and with what response time?
6. **Reporting:** Do you regularly receive an easy-to-understand report?
7. **Usage rights:** May you also use photos and videos on your website or in print material – and continue to do so after the contract ends?
8. **Access:** Do profiles and ad accounts remain in your ownership?
9. **Term:** Minimum term, notice period and rules for changing package.
10. **Point of contact:** Who looks after you, and how do you reach them?

Caution is advisable if someone assures you of fixed follower numbers, reach or revenue. Such results depend on many factors that nobody fully controls.

## Costs that are often not in the price

Ask actively about these items so that there are no surprises later:

- **Advertising budget:** is usually paid separately, directly to the platform.
- **Licences:** for music, fonts or stock material, for instance. Separate rules apply to music for commercial profiles, and these should be checked.
- **Set-up:** some providers charge separately for the start, i.e. analysis, concept and profile optimisation.
- **Add-on services:** further shoot days, additional rounds of revisions or short-notice special campaigns.
- **Tools:** scheduling or analytics software, if it is not included.
- **Your own time:** for coordination, approvals and shoot dates. Allow for it – it won’t work entirely without you.

## How do you judge whether it is worth it?

The price alone says little. Set it against three things:

**Your goal.** What is an additional reservation, a new regular customer or a booked course worth to you? You don’t need an exact calculation, but a rough idea helps enormously in assessing a quote.

**Your time.** How many hours a week would you invest yourself, and what could you be doing in the business during that time?

**The quality.** Look at samples of work: does the content look as though it comes from real businesses, or is it interchangeable? A look at a provider’s [portfolio](page:portfolio) often reveals more than any price list.

Also agree how you will measure success together, for example by profile visits, enquiries or bookings via the link in your profile. We explain which key figures are suitable for this in the article [Measuring social media success](post:measuring-social-media-success).

## How to get to a specific price

The clearer your answers to these questions, the more precise a quote will be:

- What should social media achieve for your business?
- Which channels already exist, and what state are they in?
- How much can you and do you want to contribute yourself, for instance photos from everyday life?
- Should there be on-site shoots?
- Should messages and comments be handled as well?
