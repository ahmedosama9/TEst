Many small businesses post on the principle: “We really ought to upload something again.” Then a photo is quickly taken, a caption typed, and nothing happens again for three weeks. That is nerve-racking and achieves little.

A strategy sounds like a grand concept and consultant-speak. For a local business, though, it is nothing more than a one-page plan that takes the same recurring decisions off your hands. In this article you go through seven steps. At the end you have a document you can start working with straight away.

## Step 1: Set a goal that fits your business

“More followers” is not a business goal. Ask yourself instead: what should be different in your business when social media is working? Typical goals for local businesses are:

- more reservations on slow days
- more appointment enquiries for a particular service
- finding new employees
- becoming better known in your own town
- bringing regular customers back more often

Choose one main goal and formulate it as specifically as possible. So instead of “more customers”, for example: “We would like more lunchtime guests during the week who have found out about us via Instagram.” The goal later determines which figures count for you.

## Step 2: Describe your ideal customers

You don’t have to develop an elaborate persona. Think of two or three real regular customers you would like more of, and answer these questions for them:

- Where do they live or work, and when do they come to you?
- Which questions do they ask you again and again?
- What stops people like them from coming for the first time?
- Why do they come to you and not to the competition?

The answers are worth their weight in gold, because they already give you topics. If new clients at the hair salon regularly ask how long a balayage takes and what they should bear in mind beforehand, that is a post. If guests often don’t know that you also serve breakfast, that is another.

## Step 3: Choose the right platform

For most small businesses, the rule is: looking after one platform properly is better than running three on the side. Decide according to two criteria:

1. **Where are your ideal customers actually active?** Ask in the shop and look at where comparable businesses in your region get genuine reactions.
2. **Which formats can you deliver in the long term?** Anyone who dislikes filming won’t be happy with a purely video-based platform.

If you are wavering between the two big candidates, our comparison [Instagram or TikTok](post:instagram-vs-tiktok) will help.

## Step 4: Define topic areas

Now you define three to five topic areas, often called content pillars. They stop you from only posting offers or starting from scratch every week. This mix has proved its worth:

| Topic area | Purpose | Example for a café |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Showing what you offer | Whetting appetites, informing | The new autumn menu in a short video |
| Behind the scenes | Building trust and likeability | Six in the morning in the bakery |
| Knowledge and tips | Showing expertise, being saved | Three tips for better filter coffee at home |
| People | Creating closeness | The team presents its favourite drinks |
| Practical information | Lowering hurdles | Opening hours on public holidays, reservations |

Write down five specific ideas for each area. That gives you a stock of around two dozen posts in one go, and you quickly notice which areas come easily to you.

<Callout title="Tip for collecting ideas">
Create a note on your phone and, for one week, write down every customer question asked in the shop or on the phone. Almost every one of these questions is a ready-made topic for a post, and you already know that it interests people.
</Callout>

## Step 5: Plan a realistic rhythm

The best frequency is the one you can keep up even in December or during the holiday season. As a rule of thumb for getting started, one to three posts a week plus spontaneous Stories from everyday life work well. That is a starting point you should check against your own insights. You can read more about this in our article on [posting frequency on Instagram](post:instagram-posting-frequency).

This is how a good intention becomes a plan:

1. **Choose fixed days.** For example a Reel on Tuesdays, a photo or carousel on Fridays.
2. **Produce in blocks.** Every two weeks, set aside one to two hours in which you record several videos and photos in one go.
3. **Schedule ahead.** Use the platform’s scheduling functions so that posts appear even on busy days.
4. **Keep a calendar.** A simple table with date, topic area, format and status is perfectly adequate.

## Step 6: Clarify responsibilities and rules

In small teams, social media rarely fails for lack of ideas; it fails because nobody feels responsible. So clarify briefly and in writing:

- **Who posts,** and who covers for that person?
- **Who answers messages and comments,** and how quickly? One fixed time a day is often enough.
- **How do you speak?** Informal or formal address (in German: “du” or “Sie”), casual or matter-of-fact, with or without emojis.
- **What does it look like?** Two to three colours, one font and a consistent visual style make you recognisable.
- **What has to be clarified beforehand?** For example the consent of employees and customers who can be seen in the picture, and the rights to any music used.

These points sound trivial, but they prevent exactly the situations in which enquiries go unanswered for days.

## Step 7: Evaluate monthly and fine-tune

Set a fixed date each month; 30 minutes is enough. Look at your platform’s insights and answer three questions:

1. Which three posts worked best, measured against your goal from step 1?
2. What do these posts have in common, such as topic, format or opening?
3. Which one thing will you try differently next month?

The important word is “one”. If you change topics, times and formats all at once, you won’t know afterwards what made the difference. We explain in detail which key figures match which goal in the article on [measuring success in social media marketing](post:measuring-social-media-success).

## Your strategy on one page

Finally, transfer your results to this template and hang it up where you will see it:

- **Goal:** What should change in the business?
- **Ideal customers:** Who are we doing this for?
- **Platform:** Where are we active, and where deliberately not?
- **Topic areas:** Our three to five pillars
- **Rhythm:** Which formats on which days?
- **Responsible:** Who posts, who replies?
- **Key figures:** Which two to three values do we look at each month?
- **Next review date:** Enter the date

You don’t need more than that to begin with. A simple strategy that you put into practice beats any sophisticated one that sits in a drawer. And if you notice that you lack the time or the outside view for individual steps, you can take a look at [how working with us works](page:process).
